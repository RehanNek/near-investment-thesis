NEAR investment thesis

Thesis

NEAR is an L1 with two fast-growing businesses behind its token. Revenue growth, combined with buybacks, burns and staking, makes it a solid long-term investment. The two businesses are NEAR Intents, a cross-chain swap network, and NEAR AI, a private AI cloud. Intents brings the revenue: it now earns 95% of NEAR's protocol revenue, against 34% a year earlier, and in Oct 2026 that revenue was 5.2× its level a year before. NEAR AI is earlier and publishes no revenue yet, but its usage is growing fast: it served 225.2B tokens on OpenRouter in the week to Nov 8, 2026, 16× its first full week. Burns and buybacks tie that revenue to the token: L1 fees are burned and Intents fees buy NEAR back, and together they offset 19.5% of new NEAR in 2026 Q3, against 1.8% a year earlier. If Intents revenue keeps growing, even at a fading rate, they would offset all of it by Dec 2028. Staking locks NEAR up: on Nov 14, 2026, 45.0% of all NEAR was staked to secure the L1, and 1.5M NEAR was staked with NEAR AI, which opened staking for AI credits on Jul 30, 2026. New supply is also falling: the cap on new issuance has been cut from 5% to 2.5% a year. We are long-term bullish. This report sets out the evidence for that view, the evidence against it, and what would change our mind.

The problem NEAR solves

NEAR was built as an L1: a base-layer blockchain designed to be fast and cheap, and to scale by splitting its work across shards. Fees still average about 1 cent a transaction. It found early traction. Apps such as Sweat, HOT and KaiChing took off on it, and in Jan 2025 the chain carried 7.2M transactions a day.

But blockspace has become a commodity. Many chains now offer fast, cheap transactions, and cheap transactions earn little: in Oct 2026, all of NEAR's L1 gas fees came to $223.6K. The traffic did not last either. One app, KaiChing, sent 65% of all L1 transactions in Mar 2026, and when it stopped the next month, L1 traffic fell with it. By Oct 2026 the L1 carried 729.9K transactions a day.

So NEAR has taken on two more problems: fragmented blockchains and centralised AI.

Fragmented blockchains. Crypto is spread across hundreds of blockchains, each with its own assets and apps. Moving money between them usually takes several steps, a bridge, and a fee at each one. NEAR Intents turns that into one step: a user says what they have and what they want, competing market makers fill the order, and NEAR settles the result. In Oct 2026, Intents handled $3.99B of swaps, 2.3× its level a year earlier. On 1Click, the Intents channel with public swap-by-swap data, 269K users swapped through 352 apps, starting from 37 chains.

Centralised AI. A few companies run the most capable AI models, and they see everything people send them. NEAR AI runs open models inside secure hardware, so no one, not even NEAR AI, can read the prompts. Customers pay per use, or stake NEAR and receive AI credits. It is the newest business. On OpenRouter, a marketplace for AI models, it served 225.2B tokens (units of text) in the week to Nov 8, 2026, 16× its first full week, and Brave's AI assistant has offered a model running on it since Oct 15, 2026.

That makes NEAR three businesses on one token: the L1, NEAR Intents and NEAR AI. The rest of this paper breaks down each business, shows how it contributes to the NEAR token, and explains why we think that makes NEAR a good investment.

NEAR's three layers

NEAR now works as three businesses, each built on the one below it. People, apps and AI agents come in at the top, and every layer connects to the NEAR token.

The layers are built to feed each other. AI agents that need to pay or trade across chains can do it through Intents, and every Intents swap settles on the L1.

Why the NEAR token has value

The token gains when NEAR is taken out of circulation, or locked up, faster than new NEAR is issued. Four mechanisms do that, and each one started or changed in the last 12 months.

  • Fees and burns. Every L1 transaction pays a fee in NEAR. Until Nov 5, 2026, 30% of each L1 fee went to the contract being called and the rest was burned. Since then, all of it is burned.
  • Buybacks. NEAR keeps a share of the fees on Intents swaps. Since Feb 23, 2026, that share buys NEAR on the market.
  • Staking. NEAR is staked with validators to secure the chain: 589.8M NEAR, 45.0% of all NEAR, on Nov 14, 2026. Since Jul 30, 2026, holders can also stake with NEAR AI and receive AI credits. Staked NEAR is locked, not removed, and can be withdrawn.
  • Less new supply. On Oct 30, 2025, NEAR cut its cap on new issuance from 5% to 2.5% a year. A second cut, HSP-029, passed on Nov 2, 2026 and would lower it to 1.6% by Jan 2029.

How much it adds up to. Burns and buybacks offset 19.5% of the new NEAR issued in 2026 Q3, against 1.8% a year earlier. Counting NEAR newly staked with NEAR AI, the figure reached 43% in Aug 2026 and 45% in Oct 2026. Most new NEAR is still not offset, so the case depends on revenue continuing to grow.

Where it could go. Measured in NEAR, Intents revenue grew at a yearly rate of 116% between 2026 Q1 and 2026 Q3. If that growth halves every 2 years, burns and buybacks offset all new NEAR by Dec 2028. If it holds, they get there by Aug 2028. If revenue stops growing, they do not get there within the 6 years we project. Without HSP-029, the first of those dates moves to Mar 2030. These are projections from a short history, not forecasts. The assumptions are in METHODS.md.

queries/l1_activity.sql (NEAR BigQuery)

-- L1 activity since 2025-01-01, from one scan of the transactions table (two result shapes):
--   level = 'daily':    one row per day with total transactions, distinct signers (proxy for
--                       daily active accounts), transactions sent directly to intents.near,
--                       and NEAR burnt converting transactions to receipts.
--   level = 'receiver': one row per month and receiving account (top 25 per month), to see
--                       which apps drive L1 activity and which large app left in early 2026.
-- GROUPING SETS lets both come from a single pass over four columns, which roughly halves the
-- bytes scanned compared with two separate queries. Longer L1 history (2020+) comes from NearBlocks.
-- Used for: C2 and the L1 layer in Part 2.
WITH grouped AS (
  SELECT
    IF(GROUPING(block_date) = 0, 'daily', 'receiver') AS level,
    block_date,
    month,
    receiver_account_id,
    COUNT(*) AS transactions,
    COUNT(DISTINCT signer_account_id) AS signers,
    COUNTIF(receiver_account_id = 'intents.near') AS tx_to_intents,
    COUNT(DISTINCT IF(receiver_account_id = 'intents.near', signer_account_id, NULL)) AS signers_to_intents,
    SUM(receipt_conversion_tokens_burnt) / 1e24 AS tx_conversion_burnt_near
  FROM (
    SELECT block_date, DATE_TRUNC(block_date, MONTH) AS month, receiver_account_id,
           signer_account_id, receipt_conversion_tokens_burnt
    FROM `bigquery-public-data.crypto_near_mainnet_us.transactions`
    WHERE block_date >= '2025-01-01'
  )
  GROUP BY GROUPING SETS ((block_date), (month, receiver_account_id))
)
SELECT *
FROM grouped
WHERE TRUE
QUALIFY level = 'daily'
     OR ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY level, month ORDER BY transactions DESC) <= 25
ORDER BY level, block_date, month, transactions DESC

queries/supply_flows_daily.sql (NEAR BigQuery)

-- Daily NEAR burned and NEAR minted since 2025-01-01, from block-to-block changes in total supply.
-- Within an epoch, total supply only falls: by the NEAR burned in each block (70% of L1 gas fees;
-- the other 30% is paid to contract developers and stays in supply). At each epoch boundary it
-- jumps up by the newly minted NEAR (validator rewards, i.e. inflation). So:
--   burned = sum of block-to-block decreases, minted = sum of increases.
-- Reads two numeric columns from the 32 GB blocks table, far cheaper than summing fees from the
-- 9.8 TB execution_outcomes table (173 GB). Differences are taken within each calendar month, so one
-- block-to-block change per month (the first block of the month) is not counted.
-- Used for: C1 (L1 side of the revenue mix: burned NEAR) and C6 (burn and issuance in the capture ratio).
WITH b AS (
  SELECT
    block_date,
    total_supply - LAG(total_supply) OVER (PARTITION BY DATE_TRUNC(block_date, MONTH) ORDER BY block_height) AS d
  FROM `bigquery-public-data.crypto_near_mainnet_us.blocks`
  WHERE block_date >= '2025-01-01'
)
SELECT
  block_date,
  SUM(IF(d < 0, -d, 0)) / 1e24 AS burned_near,
  SUM(IF(d > 0, d, 0)) / 1e24 AS minted_near,
  COUNTIF(d > 0) AS mint_blocks
FROM b
GROUP BY block_date
ORDER BY block_date

-- Within an epoch, total supply only falls: by the NEAR burned in each block (70% of L1 gas fees;

-- Reads two numeric columns from the 32 GB blocks table, far cheaper than summing fees from the
-- 9.8 TB execution_outcomes table (173 GB). Differences are taken within each calendar month, so one
-- block-to-block change per month (the first block of the month) is not counted.

queries/supply_flows_daily.sql (NEAR BigQuery)

-- Daily NEAR burned and NEAR minted since 2025-01-01, from block-to-block changes in total supply.
-- Within an epoch, total supply only falls: by the NEAR burned in each block (70% of L1 gas fees;
-- the other 30% is paid to contract developers and stays in supply). At each epoch boundary it
-- jumps up by the newly minted NEAR (validator rewards, i.e. inflation). So:
--   burned = sum of block-to-block decreases, minted = sum of increases.
-- Reads two numeric columns from the 32 GB blocks table, far cheaper than summing fees from the
-- 9.8 TB execution_outcomes table (173 GB). Differences are taken within each calendar month, so one
-- block-to-block change per month (the first block of the month) is not counted.
-- Used for: C1 (L1 side of the revenue mix: burned NEAR) and C6 (burn and issuance in the capture ratio).
WITH b AS (
  SELECT
    block_date,
    total_supply - LAG(total_supply) OVER (PARTITION BY DATE_TRUNC(block_date, MONTH) ORDER BY block_height) AS d
  FROM `bigquery-public-data.crypto_near_mainnet_us.blocks`
  WHERE block_date >= '2025-01-01'
)
SELECT
  block_date,
  SUM(IF(d < 0, -d, 0)) / 1e24 AS burned_near,
  SUM(IF(d > 0, d, 0)) / 1e24 AS minted_near,
  COUNTIF(d > 0) AS mint_blocks
FROM b
GROUP BY block_date
ORDER BY block_date

queries/l1_activity.sql (NEAR BigQuery)

-- L1 activity since 2025-01-01, from one scan of the transactions table (two result shapes):
--   level = 'daily':    one row per day with total transactions, distinct signers (proxy for
--                       daily active accounts), transactions sent directly to intents.near,
--                       and NEAR burnt converting transactions to receipts.
--   level = 'receiver': one row per month and receiving account (top 25 per month), to see
--                       which apps drive L1 activity and which large app left in early 2026.
-- GROUPING SETS lets both come from a single pass over four columns, which roughly halves the
-- bytes scanned compared with two separate queries. Longer L1 history (2020+) comes from NearBlocks.
-- Used for: C2 and the L1 layer in Part 2.
WITH grouped AS (
  SELECT
    IF(GROUPING(block_date) = 0, 'daily', 'receiver') AS level,
    block_date,
    month,
    receiver_account_id,
    COUNT(*) AS transactions,
    COUNT(DISTINCT signer_account_id) AS signers,
    COUNTIF(receiver_account_id = 'intents.near') AS tx_to_intents,
    COUNT(DISTINCT IF(receiver_account_id = 'intents.near', signer_account_id, NULL)) AS signers_to_intents,
    SUM(receipt_conversion_tokens_burnt) / 1e24 AS tx_conversion_burnt_near
  FROM (
    SELECT block_date, DATE_TRUNC(block_date, MONTH) AS month, receiver_account_id,
           signer_account_id, receipt_conversion_tokens_burnt
    FROM `bigquery-public-data.crypto_near_mainnet_us.transactions`
    WHERE block_date >= '2025-01-01'
  )
  GROUP BY GROUPING SETS ((block_date), (month, receiver_account_id))
)
SELECT *
FROM grouped
WHERE TRUE
QUALIFY level = 'daily'
     OR ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY level, month ORDER BY transactions DESC) <= 25
ORDER BY level, block_date, month, transactions DESC

How each number is computed

  • Revenue (C1). L1 side: NEAR burned by gas, measured from block-to-block changes in total supply (BigQuery), valued at the daily NEAR price (NearBlocks). Intents side: NEAR's net revenue after partner payouts, from revenue.near.org. The net basis compares what the protocol keeps on each side; the gross basis compares all fees users pay. L1 gross fees = burn ÷ 0.7 before Nov 5, 2026, while 30% of gas went to the called contract, and = burn from that day, when all gas is burned. Before the switch this is an upper bound, because some gas was always burned in full, so C1's Intents share is conservative.
  • L1 activity (C2). BigQuery transactions table, daily since Jan 2025: transactions, distinct signers (daily active accounts), transactions sent to intents.near, the busiest receiving contracts each month, and the two largest consumer apps split out by signer account (KaiChing: *.kaiching; HOT: *.tg and hot.tg). Every query on a block-level table filters on block_date; the two balance queries filter on epoch_date, the partition column of ft_balances_daily.
  • Intents breadth (C3). NEAR Intents Explorer API, successful swaps. A user is the first sender address of a swap, or its refund address when no sender is given. The top-ten share is the share of a month's volume sent by its ten largest users. Stablecoin shares use the token on each side of the swap.
  • Market share (C4). DefiLlama daily volumes. Peer set: NEAR Intents, THORChain, Chainflip and Maya Protocol, the venues that swap native assets across chains. Bridge aggregators and total DEX volume are reference series, kept out of the denominator to avoid double counting.
  • NEAR AI (C5). Tokens per model per day from OpenRouter's NEAR AI provider page. Revenue estimate: NEAR AI's OpenRouter prices for each model, applied with that model's prompt/completion split. Staking from stake.dao contract views and archival RPC balances of nearai.pool.near; the model catalog from NEAR AI Cloud's public model list.
  • Capture ratio (C6). The share of new NEAR that burns and buybacks offset. Total supply jumps at each epoch boundary by the newly minted NEAR and otherwise only falls, by the NEAR burned; summing those changes gives NEAR issued and burned per day. NEAR captured as Intents revenue comes from revenue.near.org. New AI stake is the month-end change in nearai.pool.near stake.
  • Follow the money. The latest month with complete data for all three layers. NEAR AI's sales there are the OpenRouter estimate above, summed over the month. The staking row is a single snapshot, dated in the chart, not a monthly series: total stake is the sum of the current validators' stake from one NEAR RPC validators call, and the staked share divides it by the total supply in the latest block header (fetch/rpc.py).
  • Staked NEAR, month by month (C6). The same two figures, read at the last block of each complete month from Jan 2025 to Oct 2026. The block's header, from FastNEAR's NEAR Data API, gives its epoch and the total supply; one validators call for that epoch on the archival RPC gives every validator's stake (fetch/staking_history.py). A regular RPC node answers only for the current epoch, which is why the money-flow figure is a single day. The public archival node allows only a few calls at a time and its operator marks it as deprecated, so this fetch is slow and cached. Rebuilding the report reads the saved file and doesn't call it. The lowest month end stands well below the months either side of it, so fetch/staking_validators.py saves every validator's stake at those three month ends, and the note under the chart says which validators' stake fell.
  • Protocol parameters. Issuance cap and the gas rebate to contracts are read from NEAR's protocol config at past blocks through the archival RPC (fetch/protocol_history.py), which dates each change to the day.
  • Cross-checks (data/derived/crosschecks_monthly.csv). NEAR price: within 0.5% of CoinGecko in every month. NEAR issued: median gap of 0.5% to the protocol rule (issuance rate × supply). Intents revenue: DefiLlama records NEAR when it is swept into the revenue wallets, in batches, so single months differ; over the last six months the median gap is +21%. L1 gross fees, ours vs DefiLlama +18% and NearBlocks +18% (median monthly gap).

What we couldn't compute

  • NEAR AI Cloud's revenue and total usage. Its usage and billing APIs need an organisation key, and NEAR AI publishes neither figure. We see OpenRouter traffic and on-chain staking only, and the OpenRouter revenue figure is an estimate at list prices.
  • Intents users and concentration across all channels. The Explorer API covers the 1Click channel, about 27% of Intents volume in Oct 2026. Swaps through other channels are in the volume figures (revenue.near.org, DefiLlama) but not in the user counts.
  • Confidential swaps. The Explorer masks them, so they are missing from the user, referrer and chain breakdowns.
  • The whole cross-chain market. DefiLlama's per-bridge volumes (Relay, deBridge, Across, Mayan and others) are paid-only, so C4's denominator is a peer set, not the whole market.
  • Gas paid by Intents transactions. We count transactions to intents.near. Attributing gas needs receipt-level tables too large for our query budget.
  • Where all captured NEAR went. 770.4K NEAR captured as Intents revenue is not in the three revenue wallets we track, and we couldn't trace it.
  • Whether AI credits get used. Credit consumption happens off-chain.
  • A price target. We don't model one. The thesis is about where value is created and how much of it reaches the token, and there is no reliable multiple for a token whose revenue mostly funds buybacks.

The evidence in detail, claim by claim

THESIS.md tests the thesis line in one table, with a row for each thing the line claims. Behind those rows are six narrower claims, C1 to C6, each tied to its own charts. This section sets out each one in full: the evidence for it, its caveats and the result that would change our mind about it.

C1. Most of NEAR's revenue now comes from beyond the L1

Evidence for

  • NEAR's share of Intents fees overtook the L1 fee burn in Oct 2025. Over the last three months (Aug 2026–Oct 2026), Intents was 95.2% of the value the protocol kept (97.8% of all fees paid).
  • Since Jan 2026, Intents has been above 82% of protocol revenue every month.

Caveats

  • Part of the shift is the L1 shrinking rather than Intents growing: L1 fees burned fell from $567K in Jan 2025 to $25K–$47K a month in Apr 2026–Aug 2026, as the largest L1 app left (C2) and the NEAR price fell from about $5.12 to $1.80.
  • L1 gas is cheap by design, so L1 revenue was never large, and a high Intents share is easy to reach.

What would change our mind: Intents' share of protocol revenue falling back below half. Today: 95% of protocol revenue over Aug 2026–Oct 2026, against 34% a year earlier.

C2. The L1 is becoming Intents' settlement layer

Evidence for

  • L1 transactions fell from 7.2M a day in Jan 2025 to 729.9K in Oct 2026, mostly as KaiChing and HOT wound down. Transactions to intents.near rose from 28.4K to 65K a day over Jan 2026–Oct 2026.
  • Intents' share of L1 transactions rose from 0.8% in Jan 2026 to 8.9% in Oct 2026. Leaving out KaiChing and HOT, it went from 4.7% to 10.6%.

Caveats

  • Most of the rising share is the rest of the L1 shrinking: KaiChing accounts sent 65% of all L1 transactions in March 2026, and their stop caused 87% of April's fall.
  • Transactions to intents.near were higher in Jul 2025 (109.8K a day) than in Oct 2026 (65K), though Intents volume is 17× larger now, so transaction counts don't track Intents' growth.
  • Leaving out KaiChing and HOT, Intents' share was 14.9% in Jul 2025, above today's 10.6%.

What would change our mind: Intents' share of L1 transactions no longer rising over a 12-month span. Today: 8.9% of L1 transactions in Oct 2026, against 1.6% a year earlier.

C3. Intents is a real, broad-based growth engine

Covers the 1Click channel only, about 27% of Intents volume in Oct 2026. The first 1Click swap in the data is on Mar 27, 2025.

Evidence for

  • In Oct 2026, 1Click handled 505.9K swaps worth $1.1B from 269K distinct users, through 352 referrer apps and 785 chain pairs. A year earlier (Oct 2025): 132.6K users, 48 apps and 338 chain pairs.
  • 91% of Oct 2026 volume moved between two chains other than NEAR. Swaps started on 37 chains; the largest was Ethereum at 31%.

Caveats

  • Much of the flow is trading: in Oct 2026, 34% of 1Click volume was stablecoin-to-stablecoin (up from 9% a year earlier) and 74% involved a stablecoin.
  • A few routes carry a lot of it: one referrer app (swapkit) routed 40% of volume, the top 10 users 15% (up from 11% a year earlier), and Zcash was 14% of origin volume.
  • Distinct users have eased since their peak: 415.7K in Aug 2026, 269K in Oct 2026.

What would change our mind: 1Click volume or users falling over a 12-month span, or the top 10 users' share of volume rising above half. Today: $1.1B of 1Click volume and 269K users in Oct 2026, against $561.8M and 132.6K a year earlier; top 10 users at 15% of volume.

C4. Intents is gaining share, not just moving with the market

Evidence for

  • Among native cross-chain swap venues tracked by DefiLlama, Intents' share of volume rose from 45% in Oct 2025 to 58% in Oct 2026, after peaking at 79% in Jun 2026.
  • Over the 12 months to Oct 2026, Intents volume rose 131% while total DEX volume fell 56% and bridge-aggregator volume rose 113%.

Caveats

  • The share has eased since its Jun 2026 peak (79% → 58%).
  • The peer set leaves out large bridges whose data is paid (Relay, deBridge, Across, Mayan), so the level is overstated.
  • Bridge aggregators grew 113% over the same 12 months, close to Intents' 131%.
  • Recent swings move with the market: in Sep 2026 Intents volume rose 73% while bridge aggregators rose 93% and all DEX volume rose 30%; in Oct 2026 the three moved -17%, -10% and -17%.

What would change our mind: Intents' share of native cross-chain swap volume falling over a 12-month span. Today: 58% of native cross-chain swap volume in Oct 2026, against 45% a year earlier.

C5. NEAR AI is a working third layer

Evidence for

  • Usage. NEAR AI served 225.2B tokens through OpenRouter in the week to Nov 8, 2026, up from 14.4B in its first full week (to Sep 20, 2026). At list prices that week was worth about $19K, or $975.8K a year.
  • Staking. NEAR staked with NEAR AI's validator rose from 10K on Jul 10, 2026 to 1.5M on Nov 14, 2026. 137 accounts stake for NEAR AI Cloud credits, and 84 have subscribed to NEAR AI Agents.
  • Product. 64 models in the NEAR AI Cloud catalog, 17 of them running in TEEs for verifiable private inference.
  • Distribution. Since Oct 15, 2026, Brave's AI assistant Leo runs GLM 5.3 Flash on NEAR AI Cloud in the production browser. Brave has over 128M monthly users, though only Leo users who pick that model use NEAR AI Cloud. NEAR AI also names Venice, the Government of Bermuda and Abound as customers.
  • Developers. IronClaw, NEAR AI's open-source agent OS, has 12.7K GitHub stars.

Caveats

  • NEAR AI doesn't publish revenue. Our OpenRouter estimate is about $975.8K a year, and OpenRouter is one channel among several.
  • Staking is concentrated: the largest staker holds 73% of the credits farm and the top 10 hold 95%.
  • Staking funds about $13.8K of credits a month, and contract activity has been flat at 168–395 calls a week.

What would change our mind: Weekly tokens on OpenRouter falling for a sustained period, or AI staking shrinking. Today: 225.2B tokens on OpenRouter in the week to Nov 8, 2026; 1.5M NEAR staked on Nov 14, 2026.

C6. The share of value reaching the token is rising

Evidence for

  • NEAR issued per month fell from about 5.3M to 2.7M after the cut, while NEAR captured by the L1 burn and Intents revenue rose to 544.3K in Oct 2026. New stake for NEAR AI added another 664.8K in Aug 2026 and 687.3K in Oct 2026.
  • The capture ratio rose from about 2% a quarter in 2025 Q1–Q3 to 19.5% in 2026 Q3. Counting new AI stake, Aug 2026 reaches 43% and Oct 2026 reaches 45%.

Caveats

  • The ratio is still small: in 2026 Q3, 80.5% of new NEAR was not offset.
  • About 53% of the rise comes from the inflation cut rather than more revenue: at 2025 Q3's issuance, 2026 Q3's captured NEAR would have offset only 10.0%.
  • The L1 burn is only 0.8% of issuance.
  • Bought-back NEAR is held, not burned: 4.1M of the 4.9M NEAR captured as Intents revenue is still in NEAR's revenue wallets.
  • These mechanisms are governance decisions that can change, and NEAR staked for NEAR AI is locked, not removed, and can be withdrawn.
  • The share of all NEAR staked with validators fell from 48.5% to 45.2% between the end of Oct 2025 and the end of Oct 2026, and the amount staked fell from 619.8M to 592M NEAR.

What would change our mind: The share of new NEAR offset by burns and buybacks no longer rising, now that the fee switch is on, or the share of all NEAR that is staked falling for a second year running. Today: 19.5% of new NEAR offset in 2026 Q3, against 1.8% a year earlier.
