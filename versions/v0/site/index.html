-- Daily NEAR burned and NEAR minted since 2025-01-01, from block-to-block changes in total supply.
-- Within an epoch, total supply only falls: by the NEAR burned in each block (70% of L1 gas fees;
-- the other 30% is paid to contract developers and stays in supply). At each epoch boundary it
-- jumps up by the newly minted NEAR (validator rewards, i.e. inflation). So:
--   burned = sum of block-to-block decreases, minted = sum of increases.
-- Reads two numeric columns from the 32 GB blocks table, far cheaper than summing fees from the
-- 9.8 TB execution_outcomes table (173 GB). Differences are taken within each calendar month, so one
-- block-to-block change per month (the first block of the month) is not counted.
-- Used for: C1 (L1 side of the revenue mix: burned NEAR) and C6 (burn and issuance in the capture ratio).
WITH b AS (
  SELECT
    block_date,
    total_supply - LAG(total_supply) OVER (PARTITION BY DATE_TRUNC(block_date, MONTH) ORDER BY block_height) AS d
  FROM `bigquery-public-data.crypto_near_mainnet_us.blocks`
  WHERE block_date >= '2025-01-01'
)
SELECT
  block_date,
  SUM(IF(d < 0, -d, 0)) / 1e24 AS burned_near,
  SUM(IF(d > 0, d, 0)) / 1e24 AS minted_near,
  COUNTIF(d > 0) AS mint_blocks
FROM b
GROUP BY block_date
ORDER BY block_date

Query: queries/l1_activity.sql
-- L1 activity since 2025-01-01, from one scan of the transactions table (two result shapes):
--   level = 'daily':    one row per day with total transactions, distinct signers (proxy for
--                       daily active accounts), transactions sent directly to intents.near,
--                       and NEAR burnt converting transactions to receipts.
--   level = 'receiver': one row per month and receiving account (top 25 per month), to see
--                       which apps drive L1 activity and which large app left in early 2026.
-- GROUPING SETS lets both come from a single pass over four columns, which roughly halves the
-- bytes scanned compared with two separate queries. Longer L1 history (2020+) comes from NearBlocks.
-- Used for: C2 and the L1 layer in Part 2.
WITH grouped AS (
  SELECT
    IF(GROUPING(block_date) = 0, 'daily', 'receiver') AS level,
    block_date,
    month,
    receiver_account_id,
    COUNT(*) AS transactions,
    COUNT(DISTINCT signer_account_id) AS signers,
    COUNTIF(receiver_account_id = 'intents.near') AS tx_to_intents,
    COUNT(DISTINCT IF(receiver_account_id = 'intents.near', signer_account_id, NULL)) AS signers_to_intents,
    SUM(receipt_conversion_tokens_burnt) / 1e24 AS tx_conversion_burnt_near
  FROM (
    SELECT block_date, DATE_TRUNC(block_date, MONTH) AS month, receiver_account_id,
           signer_account_id, receipt_conversion_tokens_burnt
    FROM `bigquery-public-data.crypto_near_mainnet_us.transactions`
    WHERE block_date >= '2025-01-01'
  )
  GROUP BY GROUPING SETS ((block_date), (month, receiver_account_id))
)
SELECT *
FROM grouped
WHERE TRUE
QUALIFY level = 'daily'
     OR ROW_NUMBER() OVER (PARTITION BY level, month ORDER BY transactions DESC) <= 25
ORDER BY level, block_date, month, transactions DESC

-- Daily NEAR burned and NEAR minted since 2025-01-01, from block-to-block changes in total supply.
-- Within an epoch, total supply only falls: by the NEAR burned in each block (70% of L1 gas fees;
-- the other 30% is paid to contract developers and stays in supply). At each epoch boundary it
-- jumps up by the newly minted NEAR (validator rewards, i.e. inflation). So:
--   burned = sum of block-to-block decreases, minted = sum of increases.
-- Reads two numeric columns from the 32 GB blocks table, far cheaper than summing fees from the
-- 9.8 TB execution_outcomes table (173 GB). Differences are taken within each calendar month, so one
-- block-to-block change per month (the first block of the month) is not counted.
-- Used for: C1 (L1 side of the revenue mix: burned NEAR) and C6 (burn and issuance in the capture ratio).
WITH b AS (
  SELECT
    block_date,
    total_supply - LAG(total_supply) OVER (PARTITION BY DATE_TRUNC(block_date, MONTH) ORDER BY block_height) AS d
  FROM `bigquery-public-data.crypto_near_mainnet_us.blocks`
  WHERE block_date >= '2025-01-01'
)
SELECT
  block_date,
  SUM(IF(d < 0, -d, 0)) / 1e24 AS burned_near,
  SUM(IF(d > 0, d, 0)) / 1e24 AS minted_near,
  COUNTIF(d > 0) AS mint_blocks
FROM b
GROUP BY block_date
ORDER BY block_date

-- Within an epoch, total supply only falls: by the NEAR burned in each block (70% of L1 gas fees;
-- the other 30% is paid to contract developers and stays in supply). At each epoch boundary it
-- jumps up by the newly minted NEAR (validator rewards, i.e. inflation). So:
--   burned = sum of block-to-block decreases, minted = sum of increases.
-- Reads two numeric columns from the 32 GB blocks table, far cheaper than summing fees from the
-- 9.8 TB execution_outcomes table (173 GB). Differences are taken within each calendar month, so one
-- block-to-block change per month (the first block of the month) is not counted.
-- Used for: C1 (L1 side of the revenue mix: burned NEAR) and C6 (burn and issuance in the capture ratio).
WITH b AS (
  SELECT
    block_date,
    total_supply - LAG(total_supply) OVER (PARTITION BY DATE_TRUNC(block_date, MONTH) ORDER BY block_height) AS d
  FROM `bigquery-public-data.crypto_near_mainnet_us.blocks`
  WHERE block_date >= '2025-01-01'
)
SELECT
  block_date,
  SUM(IF(d < 0, -d, 0)) / 1e24 AS burned_near,
  SUM(IF(d > 0, d, 0)) / 1e24 AS minted_near,
  COUNTIF(d > 0) AS mint_blocks
FROM b
GROUP BY block_date
ORDER BY block_date

Part 4

Verdict and what we're watching

Long-term bullish. The data supports the first half of the thesis outright. NEAR's revenue no longer depends on the L1: Intents produced 95% of it over Aug 2026–Oct 2026, against 34% a year earlier. Intents isn't just riding the market either. It handled 58% of native cross-chain swap volume in Oct 2026, and its volume grew 131% over the year while total DEX volume fell 56%. NEAR AI is the smallest and newest layer, but it is real: 299.8B tokens a week of paid inference, a production integration in Brave, and 1.4M NEAR staked for credits since staking opened on Jul 30, 2026.

The second half of the thesis, value reaching the token, is a trajectory rather than a fact. Burns and Intents revenue offset 19.5% of new NEAR in 2026 Q3, up from 1.8% a year earlier, but about 53% of that rise came from cutting issuance rather than from more revenue. Most new NEAR is still not offset, and the NEAR bought back is held, not burned.

We lean bullish because of timing. Every mechanism in Part 3 switched on within the last 12 months. The full gas burn has only been live since Nov 5, 2026, HSP-029's cut to 1.6% is not yet in force, and the revenue these mechanisms act on is still growing. In the base scenario, Intents revenue starts at its recent growth rate (115% a year in NEAR terms) and that growth halves every 2 years; with HSP-029, burns and revenue then fully offset new supply by Jan 2029. If the recent rate holds, that comes by Aug 2028; with no growth, it never does. The counterpoints we weigh most are concentration (one referrer app routed 40% of 1Click volume, and one staker holds 73% of the AI credits farm) and governance, since a vote can change any of these mechanisms. Those, and the signposts below, are what would change our view.

Key risks

  • Competition. Cross-chain swaps have many venues (bridges, aggregators, THORChain, Chainflip) and wallets can switch routes cheaply, so Intents' share can fall as fast as it rose.
  • Governance. Buybacks, the fee split and HSP-029 are governance decisions. They can be changed or reversed, and HSP-029 still needs a protocol upgrade.
  • AI execution. NEAR AI doesn't publish revenue. Its measurable usage sits in a few models on one channel, and AI staking sits with a few accounts.
  • Regulation. Much of Intents' flow is stablecoins and privacy coins. Rules on cross-chain swaps, front-end apps or privacy assets could restrict it.
